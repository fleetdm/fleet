How this is measured

Energy (macOS): Apple silicon Macs, laptops and desktops alike, keep a running total of the energy the whole system has used since boot. The DEX - Sustainability - Mac energy report reads it every hour, and each refresh turns the difference since the last reading into kWh, spread over the days it covers. The Mac's own uptime times each reading, so a result Fleet receives late still counts once. A reboot starts the total over. Energy totals start after the second reading. Until then, the page shows each Mac's average power since boot.

Power now (macOS): the PSTR sensor, the same total system power reading Activity Monitor and SAP's Power Monitor app use.

Windows: Windows doesn't keep an energy total that a query can read. The DEX - Sustainability - Windows power report records the battery's draw while a laptop is unplugged, and the dashboard averages those readings over days. Sleep and display-off timeouts come from the active power plan: Group Policy or MDM first, then the plan's own value, then the OEM and Windows defaults. Sleep counts come from Kernel-Power events 42 and 506.

Linux: the CPU energy counter (RAPL) wraps around every hour or so, faster than this page refreshes, so it can't give kWh. On AMD APUs, the amdgpu sensor reports package power, and the page averages those readings. NVIDIA GPUs need nvidia-smi from a script.

Carbon: kWh times the carbon intensity of electricity in the computer's country, from Fleet's GeoIP lookup. It's an estimate: it uses national averages, not the time of day or the local grid.

Hasn't slept: on macOS, up or more days with no sleep since boot. On Windows, no sleep event in 30 days. Virtual machines are left out of every count.

Battery: service when it's under 80% of its design capacity or past 1,000 cycles, worn from 80% to 89%. Capacity comes from the Device health report on Macs. Windows reports Fleet's battery health and cycle count.
