How this is measured

Who's counted: Macs that checked in to Fleet in the last 30 days. Macs that haven't are listed under "Not checking in" and aren't scored, since their data is stale.

Age: the model year in the Mac's marketing name (for example "MacBook Pro (16-inch, 2021)"). When the name has no year, the chip's release year is used. A Mac can't be newer than its chip.

Chip generation: Intel Macs count as older than any Apple silicon.

Memory strain: from the DEX - Hardware experience - Device health report. Its swap tier counts swap-ins since the last restart, so a long uptime alone pushes it to "severe." A day counts as strained only when swap is severe within 7 days of a restart, or elevated within 2 days. Strain is sustained when at least 5 days were recorded and half of them, and at least 3, were strained.

Battery: needs service when capacity is under 60% of design or macOS reports "Service recommended." Degraded (60–80%) is a watch signal.

Verdicts: a chip 3+ generations old plus a battery that needs service, or plus sustained strain, makes a refresh candidate. Battery service on a newer chip is a repair. Sustained strain on a newer chip means investigate the workload or RAM. A signal that isn't sustained yet is "Watch." An older chip with no signals is "Defer OK."

Warranty: the custom host vital, when it's set.
