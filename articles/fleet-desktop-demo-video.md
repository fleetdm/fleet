Most device management demos show the IT admin's view. This one shows the other side: what an employee sees when their laptop needs attention.

Fleet Desktop is the menu bar app that runs on a managed device and gives the person using it visibility into their own device's status, plus a way to fix problems themselves instead of waiting on a ticket.

## What the video shows

The demo follows a specific example: a device that doesn't have disk encryption turned on. Fleet Desktop surfaces that as a self-remediation workflow, walking the employee through turning on encryption themselves, rather than IT reaching out, opening a ticket, or remoting into the machine.

That matters for two reasons. First, transparency: people are less suspicious of device management when they can see what's being checked and why, instead of a black box running in the background. Second, speed: a self-remediation prompt gets resolved in minutes. A ticket queue does not.

## Why self-remediation is worth the two minutes

If your team is fielding the same "why is my laptop flagged" questions over and over, or if rollout friction is your biggest blocker to getting Fleet installed everywhere, this is a good one to watch. It's also a good video to share with employees directly, since it's built from their point of view rather than IT's.
